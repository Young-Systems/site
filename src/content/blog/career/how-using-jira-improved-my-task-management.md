## What is Jira?
Jira is a product by Atlassian that is used for project management and issue-tracking. It is widely used for agile workflows such as **Scrum** and **Kanban** and is what a lot of development-oriented companies use to track bugs, ideas, etc. It can also be used for individual contributors to structure their thoughts.

## Why not just write it down?
I used to do this a lot. However, I found that writing down ideas can lead to misplaced data and overall difficulty finding the idea. In addition, you'd have to bring a pen & paper with you anywhere you went, and that's not always ideal. Jira fixes these issues by having an app on my phone or computer that I can use to create the items associated with my ideas that also allows me to easily search my ideas.

## Why use Jira over another platform?
I am a bit biased. I've used Jira my entire IT career. It is a useful tool with a lot of potential. Ultimately, I find the way Jira approaches the agile workflow at a reasonable cost (free.99 for up to ten users!) as well as unique identifiers per item most appealing. 
> An example of a unique identifier would be: **PER-137** for my **Personal** project. As there's a unique identifier I am able to directly link this work item to other projects or in a work environment, I can share this item with a colleague. 

I also find Jira to be the least taxing to utilize. In my experience, other platforms such as ClickUp get very slow the more that you use it. In addition, there are a LOT of applications (with most apps offering free versions up to the ten user limit) that can be installed into your Jira instance that allow you to customize the platform to your liking without the paywall. 

For instance, I am able to utilize **ScriptRunner** to automate workflows without hitting my automation limits on the free plan. 

## How did Jira improve my efficiency?
I am a very visual person. Being able to build Epics for a top-level project, tasks for sections of that project, and subtasks for steps to take to complete the section itches my brain in the best way. The structure helps me break large projects become manageable next steps. When utilizing the free Jira subscription, I am able to create new work types which allow me to associate custom fields and workflows to that task type. 
> A great example would be an **Article** task. I am able to visually tell the difference between the work types, and utilize the custom fields/workflows in meaningful ways. I updated this task type to have a "Publish Date and Time" field. With this field, I can use a 3rd-party Jira **Calendar** app (Calendar for Jira by Teamlead) to query this field and have a visual representation of when each article is intended to be published allowing me to spot gaps in my planned schedule.  
>
> In addition, I have a separate workflow from my other work types that allow me to specify whether this task is: **In Progress**, **Waiting to Publish**, and **Published** allowing me to immediately distinguish unfinished and ready content. I can also set the status to **Cancelled** if I find that I don't want to proceed with this task without deleting it.

I am someone with a lot of ideas and not a lot of storage in my brain to hold onto them. Leveraging Jira, I can create an item on the fly and come back to it later and expand on it.

## Final Thoughts
I enjoy utilizing Jira for many reasons, and I hope that I inspire you to use it for your own projects. Do not utilize it for any work at your company without explicit approval, especially if you include any intellectual property or client data as you could encounter reprocussions.

With this tool, I am able to search my ideas, projects have clear next steps, and the work items have visible progress.
